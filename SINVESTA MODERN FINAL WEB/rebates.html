Policy overview

Three layers of support, and who pays each one.

Federal · solar

Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme

Every eligible solar system creates small-scale technology certificates (STCs) based on its size, your sun zone and the years left until the scheme ends in 2030.

kW × zone rating × years left × STC price
  • Available Australia-wide, for systems up to 100 kW
  • Worth a little less every January until 2030
  • Needs an accredited installer and approved equipment
Federal · batteries

Cheaper Home Batteries Program

Running since July 2025 and designed to take around 30% off a typical battery. Since 1 May 2026 the rate tapers with size, so the first 14 kWh earn the most.

kWh (tiered) × 6.8 STCs × STC price
  • Homes, small businesses and community groups
  • Battery must be VPP-capable. Joining one is optional
  • Rate steps down every January and July
State & territory

Local incentives on top

Some states stack their own battery incentive on the federal discount, usually in return for joining a virtual power plant. Most state rebates have now closed.

Timing

The same system earns less every year you wait.

Solar certificates are calculated on the years left until 2030, so the discount on your system shrinks each January. The battery rate falls faster, every six months, with the next cut on 1 January 2027.

Questions

Rebates, answered plainly.

Do I have to fill in any rebate paperwork?

Not for the federal discounts. You assign the certificates to us, we create and sell them, and the value comes straight off your invoice. For a state incentive we'll walk you through the program or VPP sign-up.

Can I combine the federal and state incentives?

Yes. Where a state program is running, it stacks on the federal battery discount and the solar STCs. The estimator adds them together for your state.

Do I have to join a virtual power plant?

For the federal battery discount, no: the battery only has to be capable of joining one. The NSW and WA incentives do require you to sign up, which is why the estimator asks.

Why does a bigger battery get less per kWh?

Since 1 May 2026 the federal rate is tiered: the full rate on the first 14 kWh, 60% of it up to 28 kWh, and 15% up to 50 kWh. Nothing is paid above 50 kWh. Bigger batteries still earn more in total, just less for each extra kWh.

Do businesses get these rebates?

Yes. Solar systems up to 100 kW earn STCs whoever owns them, and the battery program covers small businesses. Systems over 100 kW earn large-scale certificates each year instead of an upfront discount, and we'll model that separately.
